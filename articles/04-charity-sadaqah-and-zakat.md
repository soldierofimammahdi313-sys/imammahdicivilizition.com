Charity in Islam appears in two primary forms: obligatory zakat and voluntary sadaqah. Zakat purifies wealth by redistributing a fixed share to eligible recipients; it institutionalizes social solidarity. Sadaqah encompasses acts of kindness—feeding the hungry, helping neighbors, or removing a harm from the road.

The Quran emphasizes charity as worship and a means of justice (2:177; 9:60). Historically, zakat funded public needs and reduced poverty. Charity serves both practical and moral ends: reducing inequality, softening the heart, and breaking attachment to material possessions.

Practice wisely: give with sincerity (ikhlas), prioritize urgent local needs, and consider sustainable forms of support that empower recipients. Zakat calculations use nisab thresholds and specific asset rules; consult qualified scholars or trusted institutions for precise computations. Sadaqah can be ongoing (sadaqah jariyah)—projects like wells, schools, and endowments whose benefits continue.

Ensure dignity for beneficiaries, avoid paternalism, and maintain transparency in charitable programs. Mosques and community organizations can coordinate efforts for greater impact.

References:
- Quran: Al-Baqarah 2:177; At-Tawbah 9:60
- Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari; Riyāḍ al-Ṣāliḥīn
