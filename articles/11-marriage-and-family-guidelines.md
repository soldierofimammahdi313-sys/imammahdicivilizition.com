Islam frames marriage as companionship, mercy, and mutual rights. The Quran describes spouses as garments to one another (30:21), indicating intimacy, protection, and dignity. Core principles include mutual consent, fairness, and clear responsibilities.

Pre-marriage preparation—communication about expectations, finances, and parenting—reduces conflicts. The nikah contract formalizes mutual rights and may include agreed clauses. Parenting emphasizes moral formation, education, and balanced discipline.

Conflict resolution includes respectful dialogue, trusted advisors, and formal mediation when needed. Divorce, allowed as a last resort, has legal steps to safeguard rights and dignity.

References:
- Quran: Ar-Rum 30:21; An-Nisa 4:19
- Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari; Sahih Muslim
- Fiqh texts on marriage and family rights
