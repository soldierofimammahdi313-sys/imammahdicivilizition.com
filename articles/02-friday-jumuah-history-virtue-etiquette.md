title: Friday (Jumu‘ah): History, Virtue and Etiquette

---

Jumuʿah consolidates Muslim communal life through a weekly congregational prayer and sermon. Historically, Jumuʿah gathered early Muslim communities to renew faith, share guidance, and coordinate social affairs. Spiritually, it breaks routine, providing time for reflection and communal renewal.

The Quran instructs believers to leave trade and hasten to the remembrance of God when the call to Jumuʿah is announced (62:9). The Prophet ﷺ emphasized attending Jumuʿah, listening attentively to the khutbah (sermon), and avoiding unnecessary talk. The khutbah serves both as spiritual reminder and communal guidance; an effective khutbah connects scripture with pressing social and ethical concerns.

Etiquette includes arriving early, performing wudu, listening silently, and avoiding distractions like phones. Dress modestly and maintain respectful manners. The khutbah, delivered in two parts, mixes praise, scripture, and practical counsel, ending with supplication.

Women are permitted to attend and many jurists encourage their participation when feasible. Beyond worship, Jumuʿah fosters solidarity, transmits community news, and offers leaders a platform to address contemporary issues.

References:
- Quran: Al-Jumuʿah 62:9–10
- Hadith: Sahih Muslim; Sahih al-Bukhari (on merits and etiquettes of Jumuʿah)
