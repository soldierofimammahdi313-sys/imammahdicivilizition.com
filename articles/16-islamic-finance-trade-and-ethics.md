---
title: Islamic Finance: Trade, Riba, and Ethical Commerce
image: https://via.placeholder.com/1200x400?text=Finance
---

Islamic economic principles encourage honest trade and prohibit exploitative interest (riba). The Quran warns against riba and promotes fair exchange and social justice (Al-Baqarah 2:275–279). Islamic finance models emphasize profit-and-loss sharing, asset-backed transactions, and ethical investment.

Modern applications include Islamic banking, sukuk, and microfinance. Challenges remain: regulatory harmonization, ensuring substance over form, and adapting contracts to global markets. Ethical commerce includes honesty in weights, non-deceptive trade, and responsibility toward workers.

References:
- Quran: Al-Baqarah 2:275–279
- Hadith: trade ethics in Sahih al-Bukhari
