title: Fasting (Sawm): Purpose, Health, and Jurisprudence

---

Fasting during Ramadan is a pillar of Islam; it cultivates spiritual discipline, empathy, and self-control. The Quran establishes the obligation (2:183–185), and the Prophet’s practice clarifies intention, exemptions, and complementary acts of worship.

Spiritually, fasting develops taqwa—God-consciousness—by training restraint and empathy for the needy. Health-wise, responsible fasting can improve metabolic health and encourage mindful eating habits. Exemptions exist for travelers, the sick, pregnant or breastfeeding women, and others with valid reasons.

Legally, intention (niyyah) is required for fasting; the fast lasts from dawn to sunset and includes abstaining from food, drink, and sexual relations. Missed fasts should be made up, and in some cases fidyah (feeding others) applies. The nights of Ramadan often include Taraweeh prayers and increased Quran recitation.

To maximize benefits, plan nutritious suhoor and iftar meals, hydrate well during non-fast hours, avoid overeating, and combine fasting with charity and sincere worship. Consult healthcare professionals when chronic conditions are present.
